# Constitution

## Mission

Help people act in time before calendar events by replacing passive reminders with high-salience alarms that demand acknowledgement.

## Vision

OnTimer becomes the default tool for people who trust their calendar but cannot trust passive notifications to make them move at the right moment.

## The Problem

Calendars organize time. They tell people what is next.

The failure happens after that: between intention and action. A person can know exactly what is coming, plan to move, and still miss the moment because the alert disappeared before they acted.

The customer does not buy alarms. The customer buys confidence that they will be on time without thinking about it.

## Core Brand Promise

OnTimer automatically keeps you on time.

This is the internal strategic promise. Everything else should support and prove it.

"Never be late again" remains the primary customer-facing hero message.

## Our Job

OnTimer has one job: keep people on time.

Calendars organize time. OnTimer protects time.

Calendars tell people what is next. OnTimer helps people act at the right moment.

## North Star

Become the default way people stay on time from their existing calendar.

Every strategic decision should strengthen this outcome:

- Your calendar remains the source of truth.
- OnTimer handles the timing layer automatically.
- The user gets interrupted before the moment to act passes.
- Staying on time feels less dependent on memory, willpower, or manual setup.

## Category

Category: Calendar Alarm App.

Supporting category language:

- Automatic calendar alarms
- Meeting alarm
- Time To Leave alerts
- Calendar notifications vs alarms
- Turn calendar events into alarms

Avoid leading with broad categories such as calendar app, reminder app, planner, task manager, productivity suite, or life organizer.

## Must-Have User

Someone who already uses a calendar, sets reminders, and still shows up late, misses meetings, forgets to leave, or fails to switch modes at the right time.

This user is not looking for another place to plan their life. They need a stronger signal when it is time to act.

## What OnTimer Is

OnTimer is:

- An automatic punctuality system.
- A calendar alarm app.
- An alarm layer on top of an existing calendar.
- A behavior-change tool for the moment of action.
- A way to turn scheduled events into timely interruptions.

## Product Promise

OnTimer turns calendar events into automatic alarms that help users stay on time without managing reminders by hand.

More specifically:

- Connect your calendar once.
- OnTimer prepares alarms automatically.
- The alarm fires before the event or when it is time to leave.
- It uses sound, haptics, visibility, and acknowledgement to interrupt behavior.

## Current Positioning

OnTimer is an iPhone calendar alarm app that turns Google Calendar and Microsoft Outlook events into persistent alarms before meetings, appointments, flights, medication schedules, and other critical moments.

It is for people who are not forgetting their schedule. They are losing the final action window after passive reminders appear and disappear.

## Enemy

The enemy is passive notifications that disappear before people act.

The enemy is not Apple Calendar, Google Calendar, Outlook, or any specific calendar brand. Those tools hold the schedule. The failure is the passive alert pattern: a reminder appears, competes with every other notification, disappears, and leaves the user alone in the gap between intention and action.

## Primary Differentiators

- Alarm-first, not calendar-first.
- Built around existing calendar events.
- Automatic alarm creation from Google Calendar and Microsoft Outlook.
- Persistent alarms that require acknowledgement.
- Designed for the behavioral gap between awareness and action.
- Time To Leave alerts for events with locations.
- Does not require users to rebuild their schedule in another system.

## Things We Never Want To Become

- A full calendar replacement.
- A task manager.
- A to-do list.
- A habit tracker.
- A generic productivity suite.
- A passive reminder app.
- A motivational punctuality brand.
- A medical adherence product.
- A surveillance or accountability product.
- A product that invents social proof, fake personalization, fake intelligence, or unverifiable claims.

## Strategic Principles

1. Specific behavior beats aspiration.
2. Calendar reminders inform; OnTimer alarms interrupt.
3. The problem is not memory. The problem is follow-through at the moment of action.
4. Keep the calendar as the source of truth.
5. Automation is not a feature. Automation is the product.
6. Users should connect their calendar once; everything else should happen automatically.
7. Never ask users to rebuild their schedule.
8. Trust is built through concrete details, not hype.
9. Lead with the outcome first: stay on time.
10. Then explain the mechanism: automatic calendar alarms.
11. Then prove it: Google Calendar, Microsoft Outlook, persistent alarms, Time To Leave, multiple calendars.
12. Lead with the highest-consequence use cases: meetings, appointments, flights, medication timing, and other moments where lateness matters.
13. Use customer language before category language when clarity matters.
14. Avoid medical, ADHD, or accessibility overclaims unless evidence and wording are careful.
15. If a claim is not grounded in product behavior, do not make it.
16. Protect mobile-first clarity and the App Store download path.
17. Preserve simplicity. Complexity is not a moat here.
