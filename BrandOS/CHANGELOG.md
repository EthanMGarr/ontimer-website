# BrandOS Changelog

This changelog records meaningful positioning and strategy changes. It does not duplicate Git history.

## Version 1.2 - 2026-06-30

- Defined OnTimer's one job: keep people on time.
- Added the strategic contrast: calendars organize time; OnTimer protects time.
- Sharpened the problem around the gap between intention and action.
- Reconfirmed passive disappearing notifications as the true enemy.
- Elevated automation from feature to product-level principle.
- Strengthened Positioning around outcome, mechanism, and proof.
- Clarified why reminder apps are different: they ask users to create reminders; OnTimer removes that work.

## Version 1.1 - 2026-06-30

- Established the internal brand promise: "OnTimer automatically keeps you on time."
- Preserved "Never be late again" as the primary customer-facing hero message.
- Separated Brand Promise, Category, and North Star.
- Defined the Category as Calendar Alarm App.
- Reframed the North Star around the long-term customer outcome: becoming the default way people stay on time from their existing calendar.
- Defined the Enemy as passive notifications that disappear before people act.
- Added the Automation Principle: connect once; everything else happens automatically.
- Clarified what OnTimer is: an automatic punctuality system, alarm layer for existing calendars, and behavior-change tool at the moment of action.
- Reordered positioning around outcome, mechanism, then proof.
- Reordered vocabulary so automation leads persistence.
- Added active campaign guidance in `Current_Campaign.md`.

## Version 1.0 - 2026-06-30

- Created the initial BrandOS repository.
- Captured the foundational mission, vision, must-have user, product boundaries, differentiators, customer problems, vocabulary, and strategic decision log.
- Established calendar alarm app as the leading category hypothesis.
- Documented that OnTimer is not a calendar app, task manager, planner, reminder app, or productivity suite.
- Grounded positioning in notification blindness, the Last 5 Minutes Problem, and the distinction between passive notifications and interruptive alarms.
- Preserved ASO research uncertainty around App Store rank, screenshot quality, search volume, ratings, and competitive depth.
