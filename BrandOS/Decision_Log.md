# Decision Log

Record only durable strategic decisions. Do not log implementation details.

## Defined The One Job

Decision: OnTimer has one job: to keep people on time.

Reason: The brand should organize around the customer outcome, not the alert mechanism. Customers do not buy alarms; they buy confidence that they will be on time without thinking about it.

Evidence: BrandOS positioning already separates outcome, mechanism, and proof. Website and ASO work consistently point toward lateness prevention, automatic alarms, and the gap between seeing a reminder and acting.

Date: 2026-06-30.

## Defined The Calendar Contrast

Decision: Calendars organize time. OnTimer protects time.

Reason: This preserves the calendar as source of truth while making OnTimer's role distinct. Calendars tell people what is next; OnTimer helps them act at the right moment.

Evidence: Repo guidance says OnTimer is not a calendar app. The current product relies on Google Calendar and Microsoft Outlook, then adds automatic alarms on top of those calendars.

Date: 2026-06-30.

## Reconfirmed Passive Notifications As The True Enemy

Decision: The enemy is passive notifications that disappear before people act.

Reason: The strategic conflict is not with calendar brands. It is with the alert pattern that leaves users alone in the gap between intention and action.

Evidence: Existing site strategy centers on notification blindness, the Last 5 Minutes Problem, and the difference between notifications that inform and alarms that interrupt.

Date: 2026-06-30.

## Elevated Automation As The Primary Differentiator

Decision: Automation is not a feature. Automation is the product.

Reason: OnTimer's strongest differentiation is removing reminder management. Users connect once; everything else should happen automatically.

Evidence: Competitor research showed adjacent apps selling reminder, calendar, planner, task, or repeated-nudge features. OnTimer's strongest contrast is automatic punctuality from existing calendar events.

Date: 2026-06-30.

## Established The Core Brand Promise

Decision: The internal strategic promise is "OnTimer automatically keeps you on time."

Reason: The brand needs a promise larger than the category but still grounded in product behavior. "Calendar alarm app" defines the mechanism. "OnTimer automatically keeps you on time" defines the customer outcome. "Never be late again" remains the primary customer-facing hero message.

Evidence: Competitor research consistently showed adjacent apps selling reminder, planner, calendar, task, or repeated-nudge features. OnTimer's strongest differentiation is automatic punctuality from existing calendar events rather than manual reminder management. The website and repo guidance support this through automatic alarms, Google Calendar and Microsoft Outlook support, persistent alerts, Time To Leave, and multiple calendars.

Date: 2026-06-30.

## Rejected Positioning As A Calendar App

Decision: OnTimer should not be positioned as a calendar app or calendar replacement.

Reason: OnTimer does not organize schedules as its primary job. It uses the user's existing calendar as the source of truth and adds an alarm layer.

Evidence: Repository positioning says OnTimer is not a calendar app. ASO research found generic calendar categories crowded with mature broad calendar products.

Date: Known by 2026-06-26 ASO research; present in repo guidance by 2026-06-30.

## Rejected Positioning As A Task Manager Or Productivity Suite

Decision: OnTimer should not compete as a task manager, planner, to-do app, or productivity suite.

Reason: Those categories create broad feature expectations and hide OnTimer's strongest differentiator.

Evidence: Repo guidance explicitly rejects task manager/productivity suite positioning. ASO research found to-do/planner categories crowded and mismatched.

Date: Known by 2026-06-26 ASO research; present in repo guidance by 2026-06-30.

## Focused On Automatic Punctuality From Calendar Events

Decision: OnTimer should focus on automatic alarms created from existing calendar events.

Reason: The user should not rebuild their schedule or manually create alarms for every meeting.

Evidence: Website copy repeatedly says OnTimer connects to Google Calendar and Outlook and creates alarms automatically with no per-event setup.

Date: Present in current site copy as of 2026-06-30.

## Defined The Core Problem As Notification Blindness

Decision: The core problem is not forgetting meetings. It is passive reminders being too easy to ignore.

Reason: Users can see a reminder, intend to act, and still lose the action window. The strategic contrast is passive notification vs interruptive alarm.

Evidence: Repo guidance names notification blindness as the core user problem. Website pages describe the Last 5 Minutes Problem and the gap between knowing and acting.

Date: Present in repo guidance by 2026-06-30.

## Chose "Calendar Alarm App" As The Leading Category Hypothesis

Decision: Treat "calendar alarm app" as the current leading category hypothesis.

Reason: It describes the mechanism, matches product behavior, and avoids broad calendar/planner competition.

Evidence: ASO Phase 5 identified calendar alarm as the highest current opportunity candidate. ASO Phase 7 called it the single strongest opportunity candidate, pending manual rank and screenshot validation.

Date: 2026-06-26.

## Kept "Calendar Alarm" As A Hypothesis, Not A Final ASO Claim

Decision: Use calendar alarm as the strategic category hypothesis while preserving unknowns around App Store rank, volume, ratings, screenshots, pricing, and update cadence.

Reason: Automated research found strong conceptual fit but could not determine every live marketplace signal.

Evidence: ASO final findings list remaining unknowns and require manual App Store ranking and screenshot review before final ASO decisions.

Date: 2026-06-26.

## Prioritized Meetings As The Clearest Use Case

Decision: Use meetings as the clearest everyday example of OnTimer's value.

Reason: Meetings are common, high-stakes, and make the difference between notification and alarm easy to understand.

Evidence: Website copy emphasizes meetings, back-to-back schedules, Google Calendar, Outlook, and persistent alarms before events.

Date: Present in current site copy as of 2026-06-30.

## Allowed Broader Critical Moments As Secondary Use Cases

Decision: Support appointments, flights, medication timing, and other critical moments as secondary use cases when tied to calendar events.

Reason: They broaden relevance without changing the category, as long as the calendar-event mechanism stays explicit.

Evidence: Homepage and FAQ reference meetings, appointments, flights, medication doses, and Time To Leave alerts.

Date: Present in current site copy as of 2026-06-30.

## Required Specific, Trust-First Claims

Decision: OnTimer should use concrete product behavior instead of aspirational or generic marketing language.

Reason: Users arrive skeptical, and credibility comes from showing exactly what the product does.

Evidence: Repo guidance requires trust-first UX, specific behavior, no fake personalization, and no invented social proof.

Date: Present in repo guidance by 2026-06-30.
