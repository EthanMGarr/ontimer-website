# Positioning

## Who OnTimer Is For

OnTimer is for iPhone users who rely on Google Calendar, Microsoft Outlook, or multiple calendars and still miss the moment when they need to leave, join, wrap up, or act.

The strongest current audiences are:

- Busy professionals with back-to-back meetings.
- Remote workers without physical office cues.
- People who miss standard notifications.
- People with time blindness or chronic lateness patterns.
- People managing high-consequence timing moments.
- People using multiple calendars across work and personal life.

## Problem We Solve

Calendar reminders are passive. They appear, compete with every other notification, and disappear whether or not the user acts.

The core problem is notification blindness and the Last 5 Minutes Problem: the user knows about the meeting, sees the reminder, intends to act in a moment, then loses the final window before action.

OnTimer helps users stay on time by closing the gap between awareness and behavior.

## Positioning Hierarchy

Use this order in strategic work, product copy, screenshots, ASO, and ads:

### Outcome

Stay on time.

This is what the customer wants. They are not buying alarms for their own sake; they are buying confidence that they will act at the right moment.

### Mechanism

Automatic calendar alarms.

OnTimer uses the existing calendar as the source of truth, then creates and updates alarms without asking the user to manage a second system.

### Proof

- Persistent alarms
- Google Calendar
- Microsoft Outlook
- Time To Leave
- Multiple calendars

Do not lead with proof before the outcome is clear.

## Jobs To Be Done

- When I have a meeting soon, alert me strongly enough that I actually stop what I am doing.
- When my calendar changes, keep my alarms aligned automatically.
- When I have an event with a location, tell me when it is time to leave.
- When I use multiple calendars, cover the events that matter without manual setup.
- When a moment has consequences, give me an alert that requires an active response.

## Current Category Hypothesis

Calendar alarm app is the leading category hypothesis.

Reason:

- It describes the mechanism directly.
- It avoids broad calendar and planner competition.
- It matches OnTimer's actual behavior.
- ASO research found it to be the strongest preliminary opportunity cluster, pending manual App Store rank and screenshot validation.

Adjacent category language can support specific use cases:

- Meeting alarm
- Appointment alarm
- Persistent calendar reminder
- Time To Leave reminder
- Calendar notification alternative

## Competitive Differentiation

Against calendar apps:

Calendar apps organize schedules. OnTimer makes existing events harder to miss with alarms.

Against reminder apps:

Reminder apps ask users to create reminders. OnTimer removes that work by using the calendar that already exists.

Reminder apps help users remember. OnTimer helps users follow through.

Reminder apps sell reminder management. OnTimer's stronger differentiation is automatic punctuality from existing calendar events.

Against task managers and planners:

Those tools help users decide what to do. OnTimer interrupts users when the time to act has arrived.

Against persistent reminder apps:

Persistent reminder apps can be strong at repeated task nudges. OnTimer's edge is calendar-event specificity: meetings, appointments, flights, and schedule-driven timing.

## Supporting Evidence

- Repository positioning defines OnTimer as an iOS alarm app that fires high-salience alarms before calendar events.
- Website homepage positions OnTimer around turning calendar events into alarms so important moments are harder to miss.
- FAQ and category pages define a calendar alarm as an interruptive alert that stays on screen until dismissed.
- ASO research ranked calendar alarm as the strongest preliminary opportunity cluster.
- ASO research found broad calendar, planner, and to-do categories to be crowded and strategically mismatched.
- Competitor research shows many adjacent apps selling reminder, calendar, planner, or task features rather than automatic punctuality from the user's existing calendar.
- Competitor research identified Unmissable as a direct "calendar alarm" market-language signal.
- Due validates persistent-reminder demand, but it is reminder-first rather than calendar-event-first.

## Reasons Behind Major Positioning Decisions

## Why Not "Reminder App"

Reminder app language weakens the core difference. Users already have reminders. The claim is not "manage reminders better." The claim is "stay on time automatically from your calendar."

## Why Not "Calendar App"

OnTimer does not replace the calendar. It depends on the calendar. Competing as a calendar app would place OnTimer against mature products with broader feature sets and different user expectations.

## Why "Calendar Alarm App"

The phrase connects user intent to product behavior. It is specific, mechanism-led, and easier to own than generic productivity language. It should support the larger promise, not replace it.

## Why Use Meetings Often

Meetings are concrete, common, high-consequence, and easy to understand. They make the product's value immediately legible without forcing OnTimer into a narrow work-only box.

## Positioning Conflicts To Watch

- "Never miss important moments" is emotionally broad; "calendar alarm app" is strategically specific. Use the broader phrase only when the product behavior remains explicit nearby.
- "Time To Leave" has strong lateness intent but can imply maps, traffic, or travel-planning expectations. Be precise about what is paid and what it does.
- ADHD and medication use cases are real but sensitive. Avoid implying medical outcomes or treatment.
