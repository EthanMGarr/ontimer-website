# OnTimer Important Deadline Tools Suite

Date: 2026-11-07
Status: first vertical slice implemented; expansion candidates require query validation and source review

## Product thesis

OnTimer should not compete as another article that merely repeats a date. Each page should answer the query accurately, then complete the next job: select the relevant deadlines, get automatic alarms with OnTimer, and retain a subordinate calendar-only option.

The suite should test multiple important-event clusters rather than depend on one tax page. Publish useful, source-backed cohorts, instrument the whole funnel, and expand the clusters that earn impressions, qualified clicks, calendar creation, and App Store intent.

## Shared experience

1. Benefit-first snippet candidate: “Put every deadline on your calendar and get automatic alarms” before the direct date answer.
2. Server-rendered direct answer, official/projected status, review date, and primary sources.
3. Preselected deadline set with the ability to remove dates and choose a local event time.
4. Eligible iOS visitors: “Get Automatic Alarms” is primary; calendar export is the subordinate alternative.
5. Android visitors: a context-matched, clearly disclosed affiliate only when an approved program and tracked URL exist; otherwise calendar export plus the Android waitlist.
6. Analytics by query cluster: landing, tool interaction, selected-event count, calendar creation, app CTA view/click, Android offer view/click.

## Suite hub

Build `/important-deadline-calendar` as the cross-category checklist after the first reusable deadline model is stable. It should let visitors select tax, housing, vehicle, identity, health, school, and household events; add custom events; then create an alarm-ready plan in one pass. Each checked category should link to its authoritative specialist tool when rules or dates require more input.

The hub is not a substitute for specialist landing pages. It is the activation and internal-linking layer that helps a visitor discover deadlines they did not search for.

## Cohort rollout

- Cohort A: estimated tax, tax filing/extension, mortgage, and rent. This deliberately spans one competitive YMYL query, one adjacent annual query, and two user-defined recurring-payment tools.
- Cohort B: property tax and business annual reports in a small set of reviewed jurisdictions; vehicle and driver-license renewals in a small set of reviewed states.
- Cohort C: passport timing, enrollment deadlines, and the cross-category checklist hub.

Do not wait for the first page to prove the whole thesis. Do require each cohort to be genuinely useful, indexable, differentiated, and instrumented before starting the next.

## Android monetization

For the estimated-tax cluster, evaluate Keeper first because its self-employed audience matches the query, then TurboTax and TaxAct for seasonal filing intent. Use only approved program links and place a plain-language paid-link disclosure beside the CTA. Do not show an unrelated travel offer on a tax page.

Affiliate revenue is a secondary outcome. Measure Android offer impressions, outbound clicks, approved conversions, and revenue per landing session separately by vertical. Remove or replace offers that do not create value.

## Decision rules

Review each page after settled recrawl and again at 28 and 90 days. Double down when a cluster shows one or more of:

- growing non-brand impressions and top-10 queries;
- CTR above comparable pages at the same position band;
- meaningful calendar-plan creation;
- app CTA clicks or attributable installs;
- answer-engine citations or referrals;
- Android affiliate revenue per qualified session.

Hold or revise pages that remain unindexed, duplicate the SERP without earning interaction, or attract traffic that never creates a plan. Remove thin geographic/year variants rather than preserving them for page count.
